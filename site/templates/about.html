Names used on this site

For the Rāmāyaṇa, Baroda Text (ಬರೋಡಾ ಪರಿಷ್ಕೃತ ಪಾಠ) is the Baroda Critical Edition. Traditional Text (ಪರಂಪರಾಗತ ಪಾಠ) is the vulgate, the longer text handed down with the commentaries. Either can be switched off in Options. The Kannada names are proposals and have not been reviewed by a Kannada scholar.

For the Mahābhārata, Traditional Text (ಪರಂಪರಾಗತ ಪಾಠ) is the Kumbakonam edition, a southern-recension print of 1906–10, and Pune Text (ಪುಣೆ ಪರಿಷ್ಕೃತ ಪಾಠ) is the critical text reconstructed at Pune.

The two editions and how they are linked

The Critical Edition is the shorter, reconstructed text; the Vulgate is the longer text most traditional readers and pārāyaṇa books follow. Every Vulgate verse is linked to its Critical Edition counterpart where one exists, and the reverse.

Linking is automatic. Both texts are reduced to a phonetic key that ignores vowel length, aspiration, retroflexion, sibilant differences and word breaks, which matters because this Vulgate text often writes sandhi undone. Verses are compared by shared three-letter sequences, sargas are paired by majority, matches must keep their order within a sarga, and verses that don't match whole are compared line by line. Uncertain links are listed for review in data/crosswalk/review.tsv.

On a Vulgate sarga page you can hide verses that are not in the Critical Edition; on a Critical Edition page, the same switch hides the few verses not found in the Vulgate.

About the Vulgate text

The Vulgate text comes from Sanskrit Wikisource and is noisier than GRETIL's. Mechanical repairs were applied and counted: a colon typed for visarga, a final ह् typed for visarga, leftover ITRANS fragments such as श्R^ङ्ग, and entries holding two numbered verses, which were split. Verses that still contain stray characters carry a note. Sandhi is left as the source has it. Because undone sandhi changes the syllable count, metre is shown for a Vulgate verse only when it scans cleanly.

How the text is shown

Each verse is converted once, at build time, from the source transliteration into Kannada script, Devanāgarī and IAST. The conversion is letter-for-letter and reversible; nothing is retyped or corrected by hand.

Kannada and Devanāgarī follow print convention: a word ending in a consonant is written joined to the next word (ವಾಲ್ಮೀಕಿರ್ಮುನಿಪುಂಗವಮ್), and the avagraha joins the word before it. IAST keeps the source's word breaks.

The Options menu can show homorganic nasals (ಪುಙ್ಗವ) instead of the anusvāra (ಪುಂಗವ), as many Karnataka pārāyaṇa books print them. Only the display changes.

Metre

Metres are identified automatically from syllable weight: a syllable is heavy when its vowel is long, when it ends in anusvāra or visarga, or when two or more consonants follow. About 95% of the Critical Edition is Anuṣṭubh. Verses that fit no listed pattern show no metre rather than a guess.

Search

Search runs in your browser on a phonetic key. Long and short vowels, aspirated and plain stops, retroflex and dental letters, and the three sibilants are each treated as the same, and spaces are ignored so a word is found even when sandhi has joined it to its neighbour.

Word by word

In the Critical Edition, each verse can be opened to show its words with their grammar. The analysis comes from Oliver Hellwig's Digital Corpus of Sanskrit (CC BY 4.0), whose verse numbers match the Critical Edition exactly. Compounds are shown as one word with its members joined by hyphens. 48 verses of the edition have no analysis in the corpus; they simply have no button. Vulgate verses that match a Critical Edition verse link to its analysis instead of repeating it.

The meanings shown are dictionary senses of each base word (lemma), not a translation of the word in that verse. They were chosen automatically from Apte's Practical Sanskrit-English Dictionary (1890; copyright © 2014 The Sanskrit Library and Thomas Malten, Cologne Digital Sanskrit Lexicon, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0), except for about 170 frequent words (pronouns, particles, common verbs, names and epithets of the epic) whose glosses were written by hand because the first dictionary sense misleads. For rarer words the automatic choice can be wrong. About 91% of word occurrences have a meaning.

Kannada meanings are not included yet: the Kannada dictionary Śabdārthakaustubha is available online but states no licence for reuse, so it is not copied here. The Kannada grammar terms (case names, tenses) have not yet been reviewed by a Kannada grammarian; corrections are welcome.

Known source issues

The GRETIL file has a few structural irregularities, kept as they are: 595 verses have three lines, 84 have one, five have four, and two line identifiers are repeated (for example at 5.15.24). These are listed in data/normalized/ce_report.json in the repository.
