How the text is shown

Each verse is converted once, at build time, from the source transliteration into Kannada script, Devanāgarī and IAST. The conversion is letter-for-letter and reversible; nothing is retyped or corrected by hand.

Kannada and Devanāgarī follow print convention: a word ending in a consonant is written joined to the next word (ವಾಲ್ಮೀಕಿರ್ಮುನಿಪುಂಗವಮ್), and the avagraha joins the word before it. IAST keeps the source's word breaks.

The Options menu can show homorganic nasals (ಪುಙ್ಗವ) instead of the anusvāra (ಪುಂಗವ), as many Karnataka pārāyaṇa books print them. Only the display changes.

Metre

Metres are identified automatically from syllable weight: a syllable is heavy when its vowel is long, when it ends in anusvāra or visarga, or when two or more consonants follow. About 95% of the Critical Edition is Anuṣṭubh. Verses that fit no listed pattern show no metre rather than a guess.

Search

Search runs in your browser on a phonetic key. Long and short vowels, aspirated and plain stops, retroflex and dental letters, and the three sibilants are each treated as the same, and spaces are ignored so a word is found even when sandhi has joined it to its neighbour.

Known source issues

The GRETIL file has a few structural irregularities, kept as they are: 595 verses have three lines, 84 have one, five have four, and two line identifiers are repeated (for example at 5.15.24). These are listed in data/normalized/ce_report.json in the repository.
